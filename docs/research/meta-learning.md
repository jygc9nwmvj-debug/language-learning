# Just-in-time meta-learning

Reviewed: 2026-09-29. **Research/documentation only. No approved copy, triggers or product changes.**

The app may briefly explain why an activity is useful when that explanation resolves a relevant misunderstanding. This is not a preliminary course about Chinese or a requirement to understand learning science before learning Mandarin. The app must primarily **implement** appropriate introduction, practice, feedback and scheduling; explaining selected choices does not transfer that responsibility to the learner.

## Evidence and claim types

Keep four types separate in every future learner-facing proposal:

- **LINGUISTIC FACT:** a bounded description of Mandarin, such as tones distinguishing lexical forms.
- **LEARNING-SCIENCE EVIDENCE:** findings about learning under studied conditions, such as distributed practice supporting retention.
- **PRODUCT RATIONALE:** why this product chooses an activity, such as selecting a smaller handwriting set.
- **PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS:** a proposed mechanism, timing or dose that still needs observation, such as showing one sentence at the first fading event.

Confidence below concerns the named principle, not the whole interface: **HIGH-CONFIDENCE PRINCIPLE**, **MODERATE / INDIRECT SUPPORT**, or **PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS**. These are editorial judgments, not a formal evidence grading review. All eight proposed explanation moments remain product hypotheses even when their factual content is high-confidence.

Existing [learning-science research](learning-science.md) supports explicit L2 instruction, feedback and meaningful segmentation within limits. Teaching a language feature differs from explaining why an app uses a learning technique. Those reviews do not prove that a short rationale improves Mandarin retention, that first occurrence is best, or that just-in-time presentation beats onboarding.

**Additional direct but limited evidence:** Ariel & Karpicke (2018; online 2017) tested a retrieval-strategy intervention in two university-student experiments using foreign-language word pairs. Instructions combined a rationale, an illustration and a specific repeated-recall procedure. The intervention improved strategy use and retention; subsequent use with new material was observed a week later. This supports the possibility of useful metacognitive instruction, **MODERATE / INDIRECT SUPPORT** for our proposal. Instructions preceded learning and were more than a one-sentence explanation: their effect cannot be attributed to “why” alone or to just-in-time timing. Their three-correct-recalls procedure is not an app-wide optimum or handwriting prescription. [Author-university paper](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2018/2018_Ariel_Karpicke_JEPA.pdf), abstract and intervention methods checked; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/xap0000133).

## Eight candidate moments

“First” means the first useful encounter with the issue, not a compulsory popup or an automatic response to the first error. These are conceptual mappings to existing learning states, not event specifications. Examples below illustrate only three wording boundaries; they are not a German/English production-copy library.

### A. First tone — retain, qualify

**HIGH-CONFIDENCE PRINCIPLE · LINGUISTIC FACT.** Tonal distinctions belong to spoken lexical forms and can distinguish meaning. Tones are associated with syllables; a multi-syllable word does not have just one tone. Neutral tone, contextual changes and prosody prevent a simple “one fixed pitch contour in every context” rule. [Pronunciation research](mandarin-pronunciation-tones.md#canonical-tone-surface-pronunciation-and-notation-are-different) and MIT's checked tone materials support these distinctions.

**When / why / mapping:** at relevant tone introduction, beside a heard example, if the role of tone is unclear. Explain why tone receives attention; do not front-load every sandhi rule. Concept only: “Der Ton gehört zur Aussprache des Wortes und kann die Bedeutung unterscheiden.” A tone-mark acknowledgement, discrimination result or typed number does not prove accurate speech. The benefit of this sentence remains untested.

### B. First Hanzi — retain, qualify

**HIGH-CONFIDENCE PRINCIPLE · LINGUISTIC FACT.** A character normally represents a spoken syllable and often a meaningful word part (morpheme); many words span multiple characters. These are not interchangeable units. Some characters in names or borrowed forms supply sound without their ordinary independent meanings. Never teach “one character = one word” or promise one permanent character meaning/pronunciation. Wheatley's descriptions of character function and word boundaries support this bounded explanation [L1 below].

**When / why / mapping:** during Hanzi attention and the existing form–sound–meaning association, once a visible form makes the distinction useful. Concept only: “Ein Zeichen steht meist für eine Silbe. Viele Wörter bestehen aus mehreren Zeichen.” Avoid requiring the term “morpheme” or a list of exceptions; reconnect to the actual word. The explanation does not establish character mastery.

### C. First multi-character lexical unit — retain, narrow

**MODERATE / INDIRECT SUPPORT · linguistic constraint plus pedagogical application.** Word meaning is not reliably obtained by mechanically substituting a translation for each character; Wheatley's treatment of compounds and sound-based uses supports the constraint [L1]. Prioritizing contextual understanding of the unit before optional character exploration is a reasonable pedagogical choice, not a proven universally optimal order. Some compounds are transparent; useful component analysis need not be discouraged.

**When / why / mapping:** phrase exploration, after encountering a relevant multi-character word/expression, where literal decomposition would mislead. Explain the contextual unit first. An app exploration group may be a word, phrase or construction: do not call every group a dictionary word, or confuse a character with a radical. No extra character-analysis lesson is required.

### D. First active Writing Target — retain

**MODERATE / INDIRECT SUPPORT for the product application.** Recognition and unaided handwriting are different outcomes. Serious curricula distinguish their scopes; this is high-confidence descriptive precedent, not proof of the best subset for our learner. [Hanzi research](hanzi-recognition-writing.md) and [curriculum progression](curriculum-progression.md) document the evidence and opportunity costs.

**When / why / mapping:** Writing Target introduction, when the learner encounters the distinction between recognizing and producing a character. Explain that this app selects some characters for active handwriting: that is **PRODUCT RATIONALE**, not a universal scientific requirement. Do not imply handwriting is unimportant, that recognition proves writing, or that every recognized character will eventually become a writing obligation. No fixed ratio or gate on spoken progress follows.

### E. First Pinyin fading — retain, make task-specific

**MODERATE / INDIRECT SUPPORT.** General guidance fading and answer-withheld retrieval support reducing answer-revealing assistance; they do not directly validate our Pinyin timing. The architecture treats Pinyin as temporary support **in relevant tasks**. Pinyin remains a legitimate pronunciation notation and input tool, not a bad habit to abolish. See [guidance and retrieval](learning-science.md).

**When / why / mapping:** a meaningful Pinyin-fading transition after the relevant form/sound was introduced. Explain what the learner is now retrieving: for example, pronunciation from a familiar Hanzi. Do not claim hiding Pinyin always trains both sound and character production, or remove necessary support merely to create difficulty. Exact readiness and fading thresholds remain **PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS**; help and reintroduction remain available.

### F. First genuine retrieval difficulty — retain, remove difficulty-as-benefit inference

**HIGH-CONFIDENCE PRINCIPLE · LEARNING-SCIENCE EVIDENCE** for retrieval practice supporting later memory; not for difficulty itself causing improvement. [Learning-science research](learning-science.md#1-retrieval-and-spacing-support-delayed-memory--high) supports the principle; feedback, prior learning and the tested outcome matter. Recalling can feel less fluent than rereading, but subjective effort is not an efficacy measure or diagnosis.

**When / why / mapping:** genuine answer-hidden retrieval of previously introduced material, with appropriate feedback/help, if its purpose needs explanation. Concept only: “Abrufen übt das Erinnern, nicht nur das Überprüfen. Wenn du nicht weiterkommst, nutze eine Hilfe.” Never say “the harder, the better,” reward repeated failure, or treat an unintroduced prerequisite as desirable difficulty. An incorrect answer alone does not establish the cause or justify an interruption. An explanation must not delay useful assistance.

### G. First spaced return — retain

**HIGH-CONFIDENCE PRINCIPLE · LEARNING-SCIENCE EVIDENCE.** Distributed L2 practice generally supports retention better than massed practice; [Kim & Webb in the existing research note](learning-science.md#1-retrieval-and-spacing-support-delayed-memory--high) provides meta-analytic support. Returning to retrieve can combine spacing and retrieval, but they are distinct principles.

**When / why / mapping:** a genuine later return in Continuous Learning, when the learner wonders why familiar material reappears. Explain the purpose without implying the app knows an individual's exact forgetting curve. No optimal interval, required failure, fixed daily duration or universal superiority of expanding intervals follows. A same-session adjacent repeat is not automatically a meaningful delayed-retention test. Our scheduler parameters remain product hypotheses.

### H. First paper-writing experience — retain as future hypothesis

**PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS** for the proposed Paper Writing v2 experience and its explanation. Handwriting has **MODERATE / INDIRECT SUPPORT** for relevant form-related outcomes; that evidence does not establish paper as universally superior to comparable digital handwriting. See [handwriting](hanzi-recognition-writing.md), [hybrid learning](hybrid-digital-paper.md) and the completed branch research below.

**When / why / mapping:** the first useful paper opportunity, if a future product decision adopts it; current Build E remains optional and distinct. Explain paper as a complementary setting for physical writing or less-assisted reconstruction, depending on the actual task. Visible-model copying is not unaided recall. Do not promise special paper-induced memory gains. The precise v2 sequence remains in physical validation; any practice during the pilot is additional exposure to disclose; deferral is not failure. No new paper requirement or trigger is introduced here.

## Proposed presentation rules — PRODUCT HYPOTHESES

- Just in time, after the learner has encountered the relevant experience; not a front-loaded introduction to Chinese.
- One immediately useful idea, usually one or two short sentences, in adult language without unnecessary linguistic terminology.
- No motivational slogans, generic study tips, learning myths, universal effect promises or pseudo-scientific memory explanations.
- Do not interrupt every new item or force eight separate encounters. B and C may overlap; omit or combine a redundant explanation without combining unrelated ideas.
- Normally show an explanation once, with optional later recovery through Info/help **if implemented**. Neither persistence machinery nor a tutorial system is requested here.
- Keep the actual activity, audio and feedback primary. An explanation must not replace a model, introduce an assessed prerequisite only after failure, or reveal the answer during a supposedly independent attempt.
- Maintain responsibility in the product: appropriate selection, pacing, assistance and return should work without the learner memorizing these explanations.

The one-time rule, length and moment are design proposals; general explicit-instruction or segmentation evidence does not establish them. Future observation should ask whether a specific misconception was resolved and whether the interruption was useful. A click, reading time or immediate agreement is not evidence of retention benefit. No new measurement exercises or telemetry are requested.

## Interesting information that does not earn an interruption

History of Chinese writing; speaker counts; traditional names for the four tones; exhaustive radical theory; arbitrary stroke-count trivia; generic “repeat often” advice; unsupported mnemonics; fixed daily study-time prescriptions. These may belong in optional reference material when relevant. Their interest or factual correctness alone does not justify interrupting learning. A concrete useful component or movement distinction can still be taught where required by the task.
