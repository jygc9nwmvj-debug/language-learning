# Learning science and relevant SLA

Reviewed: 2026-09-29. Confidence applies to the bounded claim, not our implementation.

## 1. Retrieval and spacing support delayed memory — HIGH

**Evidence.** Karpicke & Roediger's foreign-language vocabulary experiment found that continued retrieval after initial success improved delayed recall, whereas additional study alone did not. This is vocabulary evidence, not a test of complete communicative competence. Gonçalves et al.'s review of 44 studies found a small overall retrieval advantage over elaborative encoding; feedback materially affected that comparison. Retrieval is valuable, but not uniformly superior to every constructive learning activity.

Kim & Webb's L2 meta-analysis (48 experiments, 3,411 participants) supports spaced over massed practice. Longer spacing favored delayed rather than immediate outcomes; equal and expanding schedules were statistically equivalent in the synthesis.

**Product interpretation.** Preserve genuine answer-withheld reconstruction, feedback, later return and separate assisted/independent evidence. Revisit form–meaning and sound–meaning mappings; measure which direction was tested.

**Limits.** These findings prescribe neither optimal app intervals nor an instruction/retrieval ratio. “20% instruction / 80% retrieval” is not established. Success on a practiced card does not prove transfer to conversation; test unfamiliar combinations only after their prerequisites are introduced.

**Sources (abstracts verified).** Karpicke & Roediger (2008), *The critical importance of retrieval for learning*, Science, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/). Gonçalves, Muniz & Jaeger (2025), *Retrieval Practice Versus Elaborative Encoding: A Systematic and Meta-analytic Review*, Educational Psychology Review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10076-6). Kim & Webb (2022), *The Effects of Spaced Practice on Second Language Learning: A Meta-Analysis*, Language Learning, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479).

## 2. Guidance should support eventual independent production — MODERATE for this app

**Evidence.** Renkl & Atkinson review worked-example research and propose gradually replacing example steps with problem solving as expertise grows. This concerns cognitive skill acquisition, not a direct trial of Hanzi tracing. Rey et al.'s multimedia meta-analysis reports retention/transfer benefits and reduced cognitive load from meaningful segmentation; learning time also increased.

**Product interpretation.** Introduce relevant information, then fade support. Focus attention progressively without fragmenting every feature into another screen. Guided production is not independent mastery.

**Limits.** No proof of our attention acknowledgements, exact opacity levels, 3–4 motor productions, or mandatory stage count. “Less on screen” is not universally better; removing necessary relationships can also impair understanding.

**Sources.** Renkl & Atkinson (2003), *Structuring the Transition From Example Study to Problem Solving in Cognitive Skill Acquisition: A Cognitive Load Perspective*, Educational Psychologist, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_3); relevant text checked in the [author-uploaded copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/253450162_Structuring_the_Transition_From_Example_Study_to_Problem_Solving_in_Cognitive_Skill_Acquisition_A_Cognitive_Load_Perspective). Rey et al. (2019), *A Meta-analysis of the Segmenting Effect*, Educational Psychology Review, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4), publisher abstract checked.

## 3. Explicit instruction and corrective feedback can help L2 learning — HIGH, bounded

**Evidence.** Li & Sun synthesize 28 reports of explicit instruction and find positive L2-development effects moderated by instruction, practice and measurement. This does not show that explicit rule knowledge alone becomes fluent use, or that all implicit learning is inferior. Lyster & Saito's 15 classroom studies find durable benefits of oral corrective feedback, with larger effects for prompts than recasts in that synthesis.

**Product interpretation.** Briefly connect form to meaning/use, provide comprehensible input and opportunities for output, and offer specific correction plus another attempt. Separate an unintroduced prerequisite from a retrieval error.

**Limits.** Classroom oral feedback is not identical to app text feedback. Neither meta-analysis validates an obligatory retry count or proves that withholding the complete solution is always best. A model answer can be appropriate after difficulty. The exact cue → retry → reveal policy is a hypothesis.

**Sources (publisher abstracts).** Li & Sun (2024 issue; online 2023), *Effects of different forms of explicit instruction on L2 development: A meta-analysis*, Foreign Language Annals, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/flan.12726). Lyster & Saito (2010), *Oral Feedback in Classroom SLA: A Meta-Analysis*, Studies in Second Language Acquisition, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263109990520).

## 4. Interleaving is conditional, not a synonym for variety — MODERATE

**Evidence.** Brunmair & Richter's synthesis finds a positive aggregate effect with substantial dependence on material similarity and learning material. Its discussion explicitly cautions against general recommendations for words/foreign-language material. Mixing categories to learn discriminations differs from alternating listening, typing and writing.

**Product interpretation.** Retain useful variation for attention and coverage, but describe the scheduler mix as a product hypothesis. Introduce a new structure coherently before repeatedly changing demands.

**Limits.** No evidence here for a fixed new/review ratio or maximally randomized order. Confusing interleaving with spacing would misattribute the mechanism.

**Source.** Brunmair & Richter (2019), *Similarity matters: A meta-analysis of interleaved learning and its moderators*, Psychological Bulletin, [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/), [author manuscript, abstract and discussion checked](https://www.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf).

## Open questions

**Hanzi-specific extension (2026-09-29):** [Paper Writing v2](paper-writing-v2.md#evidence-ledger) adds Kang's visual-form retrieval study and Lu et al.'s handwriting/time-allocation comparison. These narrow the application to this task without establishing a repetition quota, optimal feedback delay or a paper-medium advantage. General guidance fading remains indirect evidence for the physical grid sequence.

- Which intervals and amount of new material support this learner without excessive assistance?
- When does a cue help reconstruction, and when does repeated retry become frustrating?
- Does our particular modality mix help retention or merely create task-switching cost?
- How much transfer appears beyond exact practiced prompts? F-light task success alone cannot answer this.
