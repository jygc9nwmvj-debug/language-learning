# Paper Writing v2: evidence and one physical prototype

Verified: 2026-09-29. **R&D only. No learning-architecture decision or production change.** This is a targeted evidence review, not a systematic review. Read the [baseline and artifact instructions](../../tools/paper-writing-v2/README.md) with this note.

## Decision in brief

Prototype **selected active writing, a short supported start, answer-hidden reconstruction, self-comparison and a later return**. The strongest reason to use paper is the opportunity to practise the intended pen-and-paper production with fewer automatic aids. A unique paper-induced memory advantage over matched stylus practice is unestablished.

For this first physical trial, use two current targets, 人 and 好, with one trace, one model-visible copy and one immediate answer-hidden attempt each. A separate area asks for previously learned 你 and 我 through German meaning cues. These numbers, targets, grid size and stages are **PRODUCT HYPOTHESES**, not optimal doses. No fourth immediate production is required. The simple character may prove to need less support; do not turn completing every box into a mastery criterion.

## Four inputs remain separate

- **RESEARCH:** the bounded findings and sources below.
- **LEARNING ARCHITECTURE:** the existing contract already separates recognition/writing, introduction/retrieval and supported/independent evidence. This note does not revise it.
- **PRODUCT OBSERVATIONS:** the handoff reports difficult Hanzi retention, visually dominant Pinyin, interest in physical writing and hard-to-distinguish stroke numbers. These are user-reported observations; their causes are unconfirmed. At the inspected baseline, `PRODUCT_OBSERVATIONS.md` has no entries. Do not describe the handoff as a logged or measured diagnosis.
- **F-LIGHT:** the implementation distinguishes modality, assistance, provenance and actual intervals. No real learner export was supplied or analysed for this R&D. Current Paper Recall is group self-report, not three verified individual outcomes. Neither the difficulty report nor this sheet establishes an individual handwriting treatment effect.

## Evidence ledger

Confidence is editorial: HIGH for a bounded replicated principle or directly checked curricular fact; MODERATE for limited/heterogeneous studies; OPEN for an unresolved product question. Source access matters. No papers are copied into the repository.

| Source / access checked | Population, task and finding | Consequence and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S1 Lyu et al. (2021)**, synthetic review; author-institution abstract | Handwriting and typing favor different Chinese language mappings: handwriting can support orthographic and form-meaning outcomes; typing can support phonological and sound-form outcomes. Writing-performance results vary. | MODERATE. Supports distinguishing outcomes, not universal handwriting superiority, paper over stylus, or writing every new character. Not a dose meta-analysis. |
| **S2 Kang (2010)**, publisher abstract | Participants without Chinese experience studied English-Chinese pairs, then restudied or covertly retrieved the character with feedback; exposure time was equated. Retrieval improved subsequent written/drawn reproduction after 10 minutes or 24 hours, with a within-subject replication. | MODERATE, unusually relevant visual-form evidence. Training used covert retrieval, not a direct comparison of copying and overt pen recall. Two retrieval trials were an experimental procedure, not an optimal count. Pronunciation/communication were not the outcome. |
| **S3 Lu, Ostrow & Heffernan (2019)**, preregistered study; full methods, Table 3 and discussion | College CFL students; 60 enrolled, 52 began, with attrition. Within-person comparison of 13-minute online word-learning periods, with or without time devoted to three paper copies per two-character word. Immediate online recognition favored no-copying; paper production favored handwriting immediately and at the one-day test, but not significantly at one week. | MODERATE. Shows an outcome/time trade-off, not that handwriting is useless. Less online practice accompanied handwriting; medium and instructional allocation are confounded. Do not transfer the article title into a blanket product rule. |
| **S4 Hsiung et al. (2017)**, publisher methods/results excerpts and abstract | 91 CFL learners aged 16–20; stroke-order instruction crossed with writing practice; immediate and two-week assessments. Writing benefited form production and meaning. Stroke-order training helped order-sensitive writing scores, without a general advantage in recognition or form-only output. | MODERATE. Teach a consistent conventional movement sequence as a writing aid; do not claim every order deviation damages memory. Three copies in the procedure do not validate three as the optimum. |
| **S5 Hou & Jiang (2022)**, full text | 40 adult L2 learners; radical coloring and stroke animation impaired some **immediate** recognition measures, without significant meaning-matching differences. Separate one-week analyses did not show significant presentation-condition effects. | MODERATE. Cautions against competing visual aids; does not directly test paper grids or prove all animation harmful. Cognitive load is an explanation, not an independently established cause in our learner. |
| **S6 Sun, Wang & Yu (2024)**, authors' university abstract | CSL character learning with correct, random or absent stroke-order sequences. Correct order favored orthographic discrimination and form-meaning matching in immediate/delayed tests, with stronger visual-processing effects for complex characters. | MODERATE. A counterweight to negative/null animation findings. Protocol, proficiency, comparison and outcome matter. Full methods were not verified here; no sample count or prescription is inferred. |
| **S7 Lau et al. (2022)**, full methods/results | 42 Vietnamese university learners, ages 18–22, completed delayed copying. Familiarity and stroke count related to accuracy; associations varied with reading skill. Pauses at sub-character boundaries indicated chunking. | MODERATE for task associations. Brief delayed copying measures short-term reconstruction, not multi-day retention. Does not demonstrate that radical instruction causes gains or validate a complexity cutoff. |
| **S8 Kim & Webb (2022)**, meta-analysis; publisher abstract | 48 L2 experiments, 3,411 participants. Spacing helped learning; longer spacing favored delayed outcomes; equal and expanding spacing were statistically equivalent in the synthesis. | HIGH for distributed L2 practice; indirect for this paper sequence. No optimal next-day interval, fixed expansion factor or copy count follows. |
| **S9 Zhang, Wang & Dai (2026)**, publisher abstract | 78 college participants with Chinese or linear-script backgrounds copied unfamiliar **Hangul**, with/without animated motor sequences. Animation shortened initiation times but reduced reproduction accuracy for medium/high complexity. | Indirect corroboration of task trade-offs, **not a Mandarin learning result**. No rule that complex Hanzi need more/less animation follows. |

## Answers to the eleven design questions

| Question | EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED / limits | PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS or OPEN QUESTION |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Copying versus retrieval | Visible copying and answer-hidden reconstruction place different demands on memory. S2 supports retrieval of novel visual forms with feedback; S3 shows that copy time can displace other useful learning. | Start with enough guidance to produce a plausible form, then hide it. No claim that immediate recall universally replaces initial copying. |
| B. Immediate repetitions | No dose-response study in this review establishes a universal minimum or optimum. S2/S4 intervention counts are not optimization studies. | Trace + copy + one immediate recall is a deliberately small trial. Omit an unnecessary supported stage later; do not prescribe massed rows or force success loops. |
| C. Delayed handwriting recall | A just-hidden model is not evidence of durable retention. Distributed return is supported (S2, S8). | Later recall after intervening activity, and again in a later session; precise delays remain uncalibrated. Do not label the immediate box “retained.” |
| D. Visible versus hidden model | A visible character, faint outline, printed answer, earlier handwritten attempt or revealed screen can supply the form. | Cover **all** earlier forms before immediate recall; fold away the answer strip before starting. If seen, record assistance rather than independent success. |
| E. Stroke order | Findings vary (S4–S6); conventional sequence is a writing target of its own. | Show movement digitally before paper. Numbers indicate starting positions/order, not movement direction and not automatic correctness. No static animation sequence on the page. |
| F. Feedback timing | Retrieval with subsequent feedback has relevant support (S2); the reviewed evidence does not settle optimal per-stroke versus end-of-character feedback for adult paper practice. | Compare after the completed attempt, or reveal after getting stuck. Identify one concrete discrepancy, then retry later. Do not practise many knowingly incorrect copies. Self-check may miss errors. |
| G. Complexity | Stroke count, familiarity, prior script knowledge and outcome can change performance (S6, S7, S9). | Compare a simple two-stroke form with a six-stroke compound. Complexity is more than stroke count; no “above N strokes” rule. |
| H. Grid/support | Conventional spatial reference lines are documented; no strong direct adult-L2 learning comparison of these grid types was established in this bounded search. | Start at 28 mm. Keep field size constant while removing guidance, so size and support are not changed together. |
| I. Fading | General worked-example research provides an indirect rationale (existing learning-science note), not a tested Hanzi grid ladder. | Separate **model fading** from **grid fading**: first remove the ghost while keeping the cross, then remove the internal guides for recall. A compulsory three-grid sequence is unnecessary. |
| J. Components/radicals | S7 supports the relevance of structural units; S5 cautions against simultaneous markings. A dictionary radical, semantic component and phonetic component are not equivalent. | Later, briefly notice an actual recurring component/position in a relevant target. No radical list, color coding, invented etymology or mnemonic is required here. |
| K. Medium/input tool | S1 compares different production mappings. Finger and stylus both involve movement; paper copying can remain fully assisted. | Pen comfort, friction, scale, honest strokes and separation from onscreen hints are plausible practical gains. Superiority over matched stylus/finger practice remains OPEN. Avoid importing reading-on-paper meta-analyses into Hanzi handwriting claims. |

## Grid findings

The [MIT writing-assignment resources](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21g-107-chinese-i-streamlined-fall-2014/pages/study-materials/) establish university use of practice sheets. A [Shandong University-hosted discussion of writing grids](https://www.media.sdu.edu.cn/info/1025/26486.htm), attributed to a professor and calligraphy association member, describes 田字格 and 米字格 as established practice aids, with eventual independence from guides. This is descriptive pedagogy, not an experiment proving a grid-fading intervention.

| Grid | What it supplies | Appropriate trial use / possible cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **田字格** | Horizontal and vertical midlines; four quadrants; center and relative placement. | A quiet starting reference for proportions and left/right construction. Lines are landmarks, not strokes to copy. |
| **米字格** | Same midlines plus both diagonals; more angular and center references. | Consider if the learner finds oblique placement difficult. More lines can compete with the form or invite literal alignment; that cost is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated Hanzi-grid effect. |
| **Plain square** | Outer size/spacing boundary only. | Removes internal positioning help without changing available writing area. It still supplies spatial support and is not fully free writing. |
| **Unruled writing** | No imposed size or internal geometry. | Later transfer to a note or other useful task. Not necessary in the first sheet; can confound scale control with memory difficulty. |

**Current choice:** 田字格 → plain square, omitting 米字格. There is no established scientific rank ordering here. A general stronger → weaker → freer progression remains a testable design hypothesis. This single sheet cannot isolate the effect of grid removal from retrieval, repeated exposure or practice order.

## Writing Target selection and different progression speeds

**Directly checked standard.** The Ministry of Education's GF0025-2021 standard specifies a Band-1 character benchmark of 300, while its writing description requires 100 characters from the elementary handwriting list; the reading description refers to the level's character/word scope. At the end of the elementary stage it distinguishes 900 characters from 300 handwritten characters. Verified in the official PDF, printed pages 1–2 (PDF pages 7–8), §2.7, §3.1 and §3.1.1. These are curriculum benchmarks, **not a ratio to import**, daily quotas, or a claim about current HSK exam rollout.

**University practice.** Wheatley's MIT-hosted character notes, §1.3.1, distinguish broader Pinyin material, character reading, copying competence and a smaller set written from memory, including frequent and representative forms. The streamlined course assumes some prior spoken Chinese. MIT's regular beginner syllabus instead integrates pronunciation, basic literacy and practical conversation; it does not establish a universal reading/writing separation. These are serious but differing curricular choices, not a consensus causal result.

| Candidate criterion | External support | Boundary for this product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Frequency / communicative usefulness | Frequent forms explicitly in Wheatley; communicative topics/tasks in GF0025-2021. | Defensible curricular consideration, not validated per-character benefit. |
| 2. Reuse in useful words/structures | Consistent with a communication-based curriculum and representativeness; **indirect** in the checked primary passages. | Reasoned product criterion. No measured reuse threshold or independently validated selection rule. |
| 3. Useful structural/writing principles | Representative graphs and learning copying principles explicitly in Wheatley. | Descriptive pedagogical support; choose a useful example, not a complete radical syllabus. |
| 4. Appropriate complexity | S6/S7 demonstrate that complexity/familiarity matter to performance. | Supports considering burden, not a fixed maximum stroke count or a simple-to-complex curriculum proved optimal. |
| 5. Value of active form production | S1/S4 support production-relevant benefits; S3 exposes opportunity costs. | Benefits for an individual character/learner remain uncertain. Retain selective targets rather than handwriting all recognized Hanzi. |
| 6. Individual retention evidence | Existing F-light can describe some outcomes, assistance and intervals. | **PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS.** Poor retention must not automatically trigger a writing assignment; introduction quality, load and measurement can explain it. No causal diagnosis or automatic adaptation. |

Use these considerations qualitatively; no weighted score. No target additions, deletions or reorderings are proposed by this artifact.

**Multi-speed assessment:** Research supports distinguishing outcomes, not a fixed listening → recognition → writing speed hierarchy. Standards and curricula show that different scopes are legitimate. Allowing listening/speaking, Hanzi recognition and active handwriting to advance at different rates is a coherent **product policy**, with no fixed ratio and no automatic writing gate on spoken progress. Some dependencies remain legitimate within a dimension: writing recall presupposes writing introduction. Recognition never establishes independent handwriting.

## Smallest defensible sequence

| Step | Status | Proposed handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connect a correct form with known sound and meaning | EVIDENCE-INFORMED; already an architecture decision | Keep canonical content. Introduce what is missing; do not re-teach every dimension every time. |
| Observe the movement convention | DESCRIPTIVE PEDAGOGY + mixed direct evidence | Separate digital demonstration from writing. Replay when needed, without simultaneously adding colored components and dense explanations. |
| Produce with support, then less support | PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS, indirectly informed by fading | First sheet: one trace and one visible-model copy. No mandatory count claimed for later product use. |
| Hide all forms and reconstruct once | EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED principle; exact dose is hypothesis | A failed attempt can end in a helpful reveal. It remains immediate practice, not a retention measurement. |
| Compare and end this short block | PRODUCT HYPOTHESIS for timing/dose | Check placement and missing/extra strokes. A finished shape alone cannot reveal the order used. Stop before rows of mechanical repetition. |
| Return later with meaning/sound cue only | EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED spacing/retrieval; timing OPEN | Use targets genuinely learned earlier, with no recent answer preview. The prototype's lower section uses different targets to avoid same-sheet model leakage. |
| Reveal after the attempt; return again later | EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED feedback/return; product handling is hypothesis | Future scheduling could use appropriate evidence, but this standalone sheet writes no events and changes no schedules. |

The proposed ten-step handoff is therefore shortened: it does not require both one and two immediate recall productions, all three grid types, or a second extra model review before delayed recall. Digital preparation, immediate paper work and later recall need not all happen in one sitting.

## Curricular but deferrable?

Coherent **as a post-pilot product hypothesis**, conditional on a positive physical trial. A normal paper opportunity for selected active Writing Targets matches the architecture's selective writing and screenless-learning principles. It is not empirically necessary for all such targets. A usable digital option remains relevant when paper is unavailable or inconvenient.

“Später / gerade kein Papier” must permit continued learning, without failure, mastery credit, penalty, a blocking backlog or a new gate on spoken/recognition learning. Recognition-only targets require no paper. Deferral says nothing about ability. Assisted paper work must remain distinguishable from independent delayed recall, and self-report from objective verification.

This would still be a real behavioral change from optional Build E and needs a later product decision. Group Paper Recall cannot be silently repurposed as per-character mastery or individual remediation. No new queue, adaptation rule, telemetry or integration is built in this R&D.

## What this prototype can establish

It can test field comfort, guide visibility, number legibility, cover/fold handling, perceived burden and willingness to use paper regularly. It cannot establish efficacy, optimal counts, an individual diagnosis, or one grid's superiority. See the [physical test plan](../../tools/paper-writing-v2/README.md#physical-test-after-the-pilot) for observing these questions without inserting new intervention into the frozen pilot.
