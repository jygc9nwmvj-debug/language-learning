# Curriculum progression and separate skill speeds

Reviewed: 2026-09-29. Standards and university syllabi are references, not causal efficacy studies or our curriculum.

## Communication is the organizing outcome — HIGH as a standards pattern

**Evidence.** The official HSK service describes a new three-stage/nine-level framework guided by GF0025-2021, with tasks, topics, vocabulary, grammar and characters, and listening/speaking/reading/writing plus translation at advanced levels. ACTFL's current Can-Do materials organize communicative functions across interpretive, interpersonal and presentational modes, aligned with its 2024 Proficiency Guidelines.

**Product interpretation.** Organize progression around useful communication—introducing oneself, understanding a reply, asking for clarification—and assess the relevant dimension. HSK is a benchmark/reference, **not our curriculum**. A completed app lesson is not an HSK/ACTFL qualification.

**Limits.** A standard's lists and levels do not establish the best order, workload or scheduler. The 2021 standard and actual exam rollout are different things; this document makes no claim about which test version every center currently administers. No unverified character/handwriting count is imported.

**Sources (official descriptions checked).** CTI, [HSK overview](https://www.chinesetest.cn/HSK), undated current page. Ministry of Education/State Language Commission (2021), *Chinese Proficiency Grading Standards for International Chinese Language Education*, GF0025-2021, [university-hosted official standard record](https://yywz.cqnu.edu.cn/info/1008/1193.htm); record verified, attached full standard not accessible in this pass. ACTFL, [NCSSFL-ACTFL Can-Do Statements](https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/ncssfl-actfl-can-do-statements), current edition; use as framework, not a linear checklist of mastered lessons.

## Serious curricula integrate skills without making them identical — MODERATE for our application

**Evidence.** MIT's regular Chinese I syllabus explicitly begins pronunciation/Pinyin, elementary literacy and practical conversation. Princeton in Beijing's introductory non-heritage course emphasizes oral-aural facility while integrating listening, speaking, reading and writing. Wheatley's character notes distinguish a broader language pool, character reading and a smaller memorized writing set; [details and limits](hanzi-recognition-writing.md).

**Product interpretation.** Form ↔ meaning work needs comprehensible input, meaningful output and later use, not only isolated card performance. Recombine known structures and vary context to examine transfer. Multimodality should connect knowledge, not force every word through every modality.

**Limits.** Instructor-led/intensive university courses differ from short individual PWA sessions. Heritage/streamlined and true-beginner routes must not be conflated. These examples demonstrate credible curricular choices, not optimal causal effects. They do not establish Yale/Harvard-wide policy; no such attribution is made without a specific checked syllabus.

**Sources.** MIT OCW (2014), [Chinese I (Regular), syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/21g-101-chinese-i-regular-fall-2014/pages/syllabus/), description/objectives checked. Princeton in Beijing, [Curriculum](https://pib.princeton.edu/academics/curriculum), introductory CHI 101C/102C description checked (undated). Wheatley/MIT source is documented in [Hanzi recognition and writing](hanzi-recognition-writing.md).

**Framework context, not new efficacy evidence.** Nation's *The Four Strands* (2007), Innovation in Language Learning and Teaching 1(1), 1–12, distinguishes meaning-focused input/output, language-focused learning and fluency development. Its roughly balanced time recommendation is a pedagogical proposal, not an optimal ratio established for this app. [Author university publication record](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/paul-nations-publications/publications) is retained as a bibliographic lead; both direct article and publication-list retrieval failed in this pass. This framework paragraph records prior project context, not newly verified evidence. Preserve the existing project's use as an audit vocabulary, not a new quantitative rule. No numerical inference depends on it here.

## Multi-speed progression — LOW / HYPOTHESIS for the policy

**Verification update, 2026-09-29:** the relevant official GF0025-2021 passages are now directly checked in the rendered PDF. Band 1 distinguishes a 300-character benchmark from 100 handwriting characters. This is a descriptive curricular precedent for different scopes, not a recommended fixed proportion or today's exam specification. [Paper Writing v2](paper-writing-v2.md#writing-target-selection-and-different-progression-speeds) records the exact source/pages and separates external support for each Writing Target criterion from product hypotheses. The earlier access limitation above records the first review, not the current access status of these passages.

Research on [different handwriting/typing outcomes](hanzi-recognition-writing.md) and the curricular examples support distinguishing skills. They do not prove one universal ordering or speed ratio.

Our proposed interpretation is:

- Spoken/listening and communicative language may expand relatively quickly.
- Hanzi recognition grows selectively alongside useful language.
- Independent handwriting has an even more selective target set.

Weak writing retention should not automatically block spoken-language progression. Strong recognition should not automatically count as writing mastery. Each target needs deliberate introduction and appropriate evidence. This is a product policy hypothesis; it does not change today's scheduler or promise that every learner's recognition will exceed production at every point. Exact new-character load and Writing Target selection remain open.

## FUTURE RESEARCH: meta-learning

**Question:** What are the 8–10 things an adult Mandarin beginner should actually know early about Mandarin and effective learning?

This is a research backlog, not an approved lesson list or generic study-advice script. Candidate questions include what tones distinguish, what Hanzi represent, why Pinyin support later recedes, why answer-withheld retrieval feels difficult, why repetitions are spaced, and why some characters become writing targets while others remain recognition targets.

Before creating just-in-time explanations, verify each factual claim, identify the misconception it addresses, choose the moment it becomes useful, and observe whether the explanation helps rather than interrupts. The correct number, wording and timing are not established. Do not implement this backlog during the pilot freeze.
