# Lesson 1 — Real Prototype Test Protocol v0.1

**Purpose:** Evaluate the first vertical slice without turning the first learner into a laboratory subject.

## Before session

Record only:
- device/browser;
- input mode (Pencil/finger/mouse/paper);
- script choice;
- whether this is first exposure or repeat.

No pre-study.

---

# A. During-session automatic local log

Capture:
- task/object/target;
- response result + partial evidence;
- scaffold/help level;
- Pinyin/translation reveal;
- audio replay;
- careful-slow request;
- response time (secondary signal);
- writing hints/mistakes/completion;
- pronunciation-analysis confidence;
- skip;
- session start/end;
- engine stop recommendation;
- whether learner voluntarily continues.

Do not record unnecessary raw audio or screen video.

---

# B. Human observation questions

Do not interrupt the learner to ask these. Note after the fact where possible.

### Clarity
- Was the next action obvious?
- Did any instruction require explanation?
- Did Chinese UI help or confuse?

### Flow
- Did modality changes feel purposeful?
- Did anything feel like unrelated mini-games?
- Were any focus blocks too long?

### Cognitive load
- When did the learner feel overloaded?
- When did the learner feel bored?
- Was too much simultaneously new?

### Scaffolding
- Was Pinyin present when needed?
- Did it disappear too early/late?
- Were translations/hints easy to recover?

### Audio
- Was natural audio understandable enough?
- Was careful-slow audio useful?
- Were replays needed repeatedly at particular points?

### Tone
- Did the Tone Lab produce a real perceptual insight?
- Was feedback credible?
- Did the system appropriately say “uncertain” when needed?

### Writing
- Did writing deepen recognition or merely interrupt language learning?
- Was 好 an appropriate first recall-writing target?
- Was stroke feedback helpful without being pedantic?
- Did Pencil/finger interaction feel natural?

### Paper
- Was paper mode discoverable?
- Did the worksheet feel like an equal learning path?
- Was self-report (`secure / unsure / retry`) sufficient?

### Feedback
- Did corrections explain the actual problem?
- Did the app avoid immediate repetitive drilling?
- Did later retrieval reveal genuine learning?

### Enjoyment
- Was there at least one moment of curiosity, delight or genuine competence?
- Did anything feel childish/gamified?

---

# C. Immediate end-of-session check

Keep short.

Ask learner:
1. `too easy / about right / too much`
2. `Anything confusing or annoying?` (optional)
3. `Did this feel like learning one language or switching between exercises?`

Engine records:
- what appears secure;
- what remains fragile;
- next recommended return.

Do NOT treat immediate success as retention.

---

# D. Delayed recall — next session / ~next day

No pre-review.

Check a small sample:

### Listening
- hear 你好 → meaning
- hear name question → understand/respond

### Speaking
- greet
- say own name

### Reading
- recognize 我 / 你 / 好 without multiple choice where feasible

### Writing
- write 好 from memory on screen or paper

### Tone
- one simple discrimination/awareness task

Record:
- independent success;
- help needed;
- confidence;
- time since learning.

This delayed result matters more than the lesson-completion score.

---

# E. Later recall — optional ~1 week

Only after the product loop is stable enough.

Sample rather than retest everything.

Question:
What survived without recent rehearsal?

---

# F. First-prototype decision criteria

### KEEP
Mechanic works smoothly and delayed evidence supports it.

### ADJUST
Concept appears useful but timing/scaffold/UI is wrong.

### REMOVE
Mechanic adds friction without useful learning evidence.

### CANNOT JUDGE
Technical quality or sample is insufficient.

Do not “save” a favorite feature with rationalization.

---

# G. Specific hypotheses under test

1. **HYPOTHESIS:** 好 is a good first active writing target.
2. **HYPOTHESIS:** dense early multimodal treatment feels coherent rather than slow.
3. **HYPOTHESIS:** the session can mix modalities without feeling fragmented.
4. **HYPOTHESIS:** progressive Pinyin fading is understandable.
5. **HYPOTHESIS:** our Answer Interpreter gives fairer feedback than exact matching.
6. **HYPOTHESIS:** tone visualization/feedback is useful enough to keep.
7. **HYPOTHESIS:** paper mode can be equal rather than secondary.
8. **HYPOTHESIS:** the engine can recommend stopping without frustrating the learner.
9. **HYPOTHESIS:** the learner can return later and receive a meaningfully adapted review.
10. **HYPOTHESIS:** the calm non-gamified design still feels enjoyable.

---

# H. What the first test cannot prove

One learner cannot establish:
- general effectiveness;
- optimal review intervals;
- population-wide difficulty;
- superiority over competitors;
- scientific validity of pronunciation scoring.

The first test is for architecture, usability, obvious didactic failures and hypothesis generation.
