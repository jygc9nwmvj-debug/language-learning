# Lesson 2 — Keep the conversation alive

## Can-do
- say that you understand / do not understand
- ask for repetition
- ask someone to speak more slowly
- make a simple yes/no response
- use known pronouns/name material from Lesson 1

## New high-leverage language functions
- understand / not understand
- negation with 不 where appropriate
- a simple question mechanism
- repeat / again
- slower
- yes / correct / not correct as pragmatically appropriate

## Recycling from Lesson 1
- 你
- 我
- 叫
- 你好
- name question
- 謝謝/谢谢

## Example cumulative interactions
A asks learner's name.
Learner misses it.
Learner asks for repetition.
A repeats more slowly.
Learner answers with 我叫…

The new lesson therefore strengthens Lesson 1 instead of replacing it.

## Pronunciation
Continue tone perception/production with known words.
Introduce only the specific new sound contrasts needed by the new vocabulary.

## Writing
Review 好 from memory.
One new active character only if it provides strong structural/functional value.
Other new words may be recognition-only.

## Lightness
Use a small misunderstanding where tone or a missed word changes the interaction, but make the repair strategy the learning point.

# Lesson 3 — Numbers, things, amounts

## Can-do
- understand and produce basic numbers needed for prices/quantities
- ask how much / how many in a tightly controlled context
- indicate this/that
- say whether something is available / not available at a basic level
- perform a tiny purchase/order interaction

## New high-leverage systems
- numbers
- this / that
- how much / how many
- have / not have
- first classifier(s) only in real use

## Recycling from Lessons 1–2
- 你 / 我
- question behavior
- negation / not
- repetition / slower
- 謝謝/谢谢
- greetings when opening the interaction

## Transfer
The same number system later serves:
- prices
- room numbers
- train times
- quantities
- tickets
- phone numbers

## Pronunciation
Numbers create useful tone sequences; practice them in short meaningful chunks, not as an isolated chant only.

## Writing
Do not require all number characters simply because numbers are introduced.
Choose writing targets according to usefulness and motor/orthographic learning value.

## Lightness
Numbers make early playful micro-sentences possible. Use them only if all required vocabulary is already known or independently useful.

# Connectivity check

Lesson 1 gives:
- contact
- I/you
- name
- first tone awareness
- first Hanzi experience

Lesson 2 adds:
- repair
- negation/question control

Lesson 3 adds:
- quantity/reference/existence

This creates a growing generative space rather than three independent topics.

After Lesson 3, a learner can already:
- initiate a tiny interaction
- repair misunderstanding
- identify self/other
- ask/answer a name
- understand/use basic quantities
- begin a transaction

That is the intended cumulative pattern for later lessons.
